# AI-lab hackathon winners, 2025–2026 (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Scope: winners of Anthropic/Claude, OpenAI, and secondary Google/Gemma hackathons, most recent first.

Coverage caveat: most winner data comes from secondary aggregators (blockchain.news, a Japanese mirror of an Anthropic post, a Hugging Face prize ledger, company press releases). No official Anthropic or OpenAI winner page was readable. Anthropic and OpenAI pages returned 403 or were not fetched. The Kaggle pages were not fetched. Placements marked "search summary" were seen in search snippets only and not confirmed by fetch. Search budget ran about 4 calls over the researcher soft limit to close gaps. Almost nothing from 2025 surfaced except the gpt-oss red-teaming challenge.

## Key Question 1: Anthropic / Claude hackathons (2026)

### Takeaway
The best-documented Anthropic events are the 2026 "Built with Opus" hackathons. Winners were mostly domain experts (physician, lawyer, cardiologist, electronics repair, educator) building tools that turn documents, schematics, photos, or video into concrete decisions. Prize tiers and special awards were tied to the newest model or feature. Judges are not named in any readable source, and demo format is not documented.

### Cited Findings

**A. Built with Opus 4.7 (virtual, Cerebral Valley + Anthropic). Confirmed by fetch of a secondary article.**
- Dates: held virtually April 21–26, 2026; winners announced May 5, 2026; article published June 15, 2026. Over 20,000 applicants, 500 selected, $100,000 prize pool. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- 1st, $50,000: **MedKit**, Bedirhan Keskin (Istanbul-based physician turned developer). Gamified training for junior doctors that simulates patient cases and grades decisions against clinical guidelines. Uses Claude Managed Agents. Team size not stated. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- 2nd, $30,000: **Wrench Board**, Alexis Chapellier (French electronics repair expert, described as self-taught). Diagnostic tool that takes schematics as input and suggests fixes. Uses Opus 4.7 to interpret visual data. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- 3rd, $10,000: **Maieutic**, Paula Vásquez-Henríquez (computer science educator, Chile). Teaching IDE that requires students to state their coding plan in plain language before coding. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- Special awards, amounts not stated: "Most Creative Use of Opus 4.7" to Virtual Puppet Theater (Rene Hangstrup Møller); "Keep Thinking" to MaestrIA (Benjamin Torralbo, home-repair diagnostics connecting users with tradespeople); "Best Use of Claude Managed Agents" to ARIA (Idriss Benguezzou and Adam Hnaien, factory maintenance AI). [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- Judges are not named and no scoring criteria are given. The article's only rationale is brief award descriptions, e.g. MaestrIA's "ambitious approach to digitizing trade expertise." [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- A search summary also describes it as a week-long Cerebral Valley virtual competition focused on Claude Code with Opus 4.7. Search summary only: [startupgrantsindia.com](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/built-with-opus-47-a-claude-code-hackathon)

Note: the article lists TARA as 4th in its headings but leaves it out of its takeaways summary. **C. One-day in-person hackathon, San Francisco, May 13, 2026. Winners confirmed only via one secondary article. Event name not stated.**
- 12 hours, 1,500 applicants, 310 participants. Each team received $500 in credits, which the article does not present as a prize. Prize amounts and judges not stated. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)
- 1st: **Tekton**, Holly Tang and Austin Burgess. 3D reconstruction for digitally preserving historical architecture (Notre-Dame's spire, Tang Dynasty temples), with each element traceable to its source. Opus 4.8 verifies details through independent sub-agents and self-correcting loops. Plans to open-source it and invite museums and governments to contribute. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)
- 2nd: **Sim Francisco**, Tanmayi Priya Dasari and Tejas Prabhune. Digital twin of San Francisco with 10,000 synthetic residents built from U.S. Census data, polled to predict votes. Claims its forecast for March 2024's Prop A was within 0.4% of the result (a single example). Opus 4.8 runs the backend; residents are clustered into 300 personas to cut cost. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)
- 3rd: **Custom Universe**, Jake Stevens and Mauricio Pereira. Single photo converted to an editable photorealistic 3D scene, used to generate synthetic training data for robotics labs. Plans to stay free and open-source. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)
- Date conflict: the article says Opus 4.8 "launched just two weeks prior on May 28," which falls after the May 13 event. It also mentions a "Fable 5 system" with no context. Treat dates and model-timing claims as unreliable. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)

### Inferences
- The most-cited Anthropic winners fit one profile: a domain expert with a document, image, or video input and a concrete decision or output. Most named placements in B and C match this, and A matches it partly.
- Anthropic's special awards tied prizes to specific features ("Best Use of Claude Managed Agents," "Most Creative Use of Opus 4.7"). Winning projects used those features. This is an inference from the article, not confirmed by a judge's statement.
- Secondary articles reported rough numbers and impact claims (dollars saved, percentages) without sources. Treat them as the builders' claims, not verified results.
- The Opus 4.6 rubric (if accurate) put impact and demo quality at 55% combined. This suggests impact claims and a clean demo matter more than technical depth.

### Gaps
- No official Anthropic or Cerebral Valley winner page was read. Placements in A and B rely on secondary sources. B has conflicting dates.
- Judges and their comments are not named in any readable source for any Anthropic event.
- Demo format (live vs video), duration, and team size are not stated for most winners.
- Built with Claude: Japan 2026 results were not searched in depth.
- 2025 Anthropic hackathons: none found.

### Takeaway
OpenAI-linked winner data is sparse and scattered across partner-run events. The one large, well-documented OpenAI-sponsored event is the gpt-oss-20b red-teaming challenge on Kaggle, which was judged on written writeups rather than demos. ### Cited Findings

Fifteen prize-winning teams and honorable mentions presented. [ndurner.substack.com](https://ndurner.substack.com/p/kaggle-red-teaming-challenge-openai-gpt-oss-concluded)

## Key Question 5: Patterns and implications

### Takeaway
Across the readable sources, winners are domain experts building an end-to-end tool for a concrete real-world problem, using multimodal input (documents, schematics, photos, video) and often multi-agent verification. Prizes are split into track and sponsor-feature awards, so tying a project to the sponsor's newest feature is a common path to a special prize. Demo format is undocumented, which is the largest gap for a Meta hackathon plan.

### Cited Findings
- Domain-expert winners: lawyer (CrossBeam), cardiologist (PostVisit), physician (MedKit), electronics repair expert (Wrench Board), educator (Maieutic), former government staff (TARA). [claudecode.jp](https://claudecode.jp/en/news/child/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon) and [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- Multimodal input: blueprints (CrossBeam), schematics (Wrench Board), dashcam video (TARA), a single photo to 3D (Custom Universe), short videos (Pozify). [claudecode.jp](https://claudecode.jp/en/news/child/meet-the-winners-of-our-built-with-opus-4-6-claude-code-hackathon) and [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)
- Multi-agent and verification loops: Tekton (independent sub-agents, self-correcting loops), CrossBeam (cooperating AI helpers), ARIA and MedKit (Claude Managed Agents). [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners) and [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- Sponsor-feature awards: "Best Use of Claude Managed Agents," "Most Creative Use of Opus 4.7," and Google's "Best use of ARCore" and "Best use of Flutter." [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners) and [ai.google.dev](https://ai.google.dev/competition)
- Open-source stance: Tekton and Custom Universe both plan to open-source their work. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners)
- Team size: mostly not stated. Where stated, the projects read as solo or small-team builds. [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners)
- Category frequency across named placements (my tally of ~17 named Anthropic and Google placements): education/learning about 4, health/medical about 3, civic/government/real-world records about 4, hardware/robotics/repair about 3, creative/media about 3. Categories overlap, so counts are approximate.

### Inferences (for the report writer; grounded in the findings above, not verified against judge statements)
1. Lead with a real user in a real domain (a clinician, a tradesperson, a teacher, a lawyer). The named winners consistently do this, and it is the most repeated pattern.
2. Use the input the user already has: a document, photo, schematic, or video. Vision and document reading show up in most winning projects.
3. Make the verification or multi-agent loop visible. Tekton and CrossBeam both describe it as a core feature.
4. Use the sponsor's newest feature, but as a means to the product. Special awards were tied to it, and it is cheap to target.
5. Quantified impact claims appear in many winning write-ups (dollars saved, percentages). Their accuracy is unverified; a Meta team should use a verifiable number if it makes one.
6. Expect a $50k-style first prize and many smaller awards. Winning did not depend on a single category.
7. Demo format is the open question. The Anthropic events describe finished products with demos but say nothing about live vs video. The gpt-oss event rewarded writeups over demos. A Meta team should not assume the format.

### Gaps
- Demo format (live vs video) and demo length: not documented for any winner. This is the most important missing piece.
- Judging criteria for Anthropic events beyond the Opus 4.6 rubric (weak provenance): not confirmed.
- Whether polish mattered vs. idea: no judge statement found.
- Whether winners used the newest sponsor model vs an older one: confirmed only for the Opus 4.7 and 4.8 events, from secondary sources.
- The Kaggle, Anthropic, and OpenAI primary pages were not readable, so most placements remain secondary-source claims.
