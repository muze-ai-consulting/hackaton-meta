## Key Question 3: Most ambitious hackathon winners in 2026

### Takeaway
The clearest ambitious 2026 winner is Sim Francisco (2nd at the May 2026 Claude Opus 4.8 hackathon), a population simulator with backtests. The Opus 4.7 winners were mostly vertical tools, and the 2026 world-model and agent-swarm events found no published winners. No funding after a 2026 hackathon was verified for any winner.

### Cited Findings
- Claude Opus 4.8 hackathon, San Francisco, May 13, 2026 (12 hours): 310 participants from 1,500 applicants; $500 credits per team. Winners: 1st Tekton (Holly Tang, Austin Burgess), 3D reconstruction of historical buildings with traceable sources; 2nd Sim Francisco (Tanmayi Priya Dasari, Tejas Prabhune), digital twin of San Francisco with "10,000 synthetic residents"; 3rd Custom Universe (Jake Stevens, Mauricio Pereira), photo-to-editable 3D scene for synthetic robotics training data — [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners). Date conflict: the article also says the model "launched just two weeks prior on May 28," so dates are uncertain.
- Sim Francisco repository (Rust, axum, SQLite): a distributionally accurate population sampled from ACS PUMS for 8 SF PUMAs, with PUMS weights and Pew religion figures. Opinions are weighted averages over agents. The LLM is called only for about 12 demographic archetypes, batched, then post-stratified. Personas are seeded and deterministic, and a SQLite cache keyed by model and prompt makes runs reproducible. A visual life simulation sits beside the prediction engine — [GitHub: tejasprabhune/simfrancisco](https://github.com/tejasprabhune/simfrancisco).
- Sim Francisco backtests: 2024 presidential vote predicted 81.3% Dem vs 83.8% actual; March 2024 Proposition A predicted 70% yes vs 70.38% actual; backtest model GPT-4o (cutoff October 2023, before both events) — [GitHub: simfrancisco](https://github.com/tejasprabhune/simfrancisco). Headline score about 0.84, but the README gate (0.70) and the reproduce section (~0.85) conflict.
- Sim Francisco agent count: the repo gives NO headcount. The "10,000 synthetic residents" figure appears only in the blockchain.news writeup — [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners). Treat 10,000 as unverified.
- Built with Opus 4.7 Claude Code hackathon (Anthropic and Cerebral Valley): run April 21 to 26, 2026; over 20,000 applicants, 500 selected; $100,000 in API credits. Winners (Claude blog dated June 15, 2026): 1st Medkit (Bedirhan Keskin), gamified clinical training with an automated grader against guidelines; 2nd Wrench Board (Alexis Chapellier), AI assistant reasoning over schematics for electronics repair; 3rd Maieutic (Paula Vásquez-Henríquez), IDE that makes students write a spec first; plus Virtual Puppet Theater, MaestrIA, and ARIA (five-agent factory maintenance) — [Claude blog](https://www.claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon); [blockchain.news Opus 4.7](https://blockchain.news/news/anthropic-opus-4-7-hackathon-winners). Reported prizes ($50k first, $30k second) come from blockchain.news only. Announcement date conflicts (May 5 in one source, June 15 on Claude's page).

## Key Question 4: What made ambitious projects credible in a short build?

### Takeaway
The credible pattern in 2026 winners: a narrow, public-data core, a falsifiable prediction with backtests on known past events, reproducibility by caching and seeds, and a visual layer that sits on top of the scored engine.

### Cited Findings
- Sim Francisco backtests on past events (2024 election, 2024 ballot measure), with a model whose training cutoff predates the events — [GitHub: simfrancisco](https://github.com/tejasprabhune/simfrancisco).
- LLM calls cut to about 12 batched archetype queries, with the rest done by weighted statistics; deterministic seeds; SQLite cache for reruns — [GitHub: simfrancisco](https://github.com/tejasprabhune/simfrancisco).
- Two-layer design: a scored prediction engine plus a visual life simulation for the demo — [GitHub: simfrancisco](https://github.com/tejasprabhune/simfrancisco).
- Medkit: automated grader checking performance against clinical guidelines; Maieutic: spec-versus-code drift flag — [Claude blog](https://www.claude.com/blog/meet-the-winners-of-built-with-opus-4-7-claude-code-hackathon). Verifiable output as the demo.
- Tekton: each reconstructed detail traceable to its source — [blockchain.news](https://blockchain.news/news/claude-opus-4-8-hackathon-winners).
- Pitch-simulation and synthetic-user tools face skepticism that their outputs do not predict real outcomes — [Product Hunt / user reviews, Cogently and Gatekeep](https://ipsnews.net/business/2026/07/28/cogently-launches-ai-pitch-partner-that-gives-founders-a-vc-grade-deck-audit-in-less-than-30-seconds-and-lets-them-rehearse-the-boardroom-pitch-before-its-real/?amp=1).

### Inferences
- For Meta's 9-day window: pick one prediction or evaluation target, build a backtest or a held-out check, and make one scored output visible on screen.
- Avoid claiming population-scale fidelity without numbers. The judges' likely check is "does it match something that already happened."

### Gaps
- No public judging rubric from Meta beyond the two criteria per track.
- No 2026 post-mortems from winners on what judges asked.
